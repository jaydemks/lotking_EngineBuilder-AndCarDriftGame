# Release Notes: v0.7.5

## Lot King Engine Builder v0.7.5 - Asset Scout, First-Person Gameplay and Character Systems

### Release status

- Status: released.
- Current public release: `v0.7.5`.
- Three.js baseline: pinned local r185 compatibility bundle.
- This document records the work completed for the v0.7.5 release.

### Asset Scout — free online asset search

- Added **Asset Scout**, a button docked under the Inspector sidebar that opens a panel to search free online asset catalogues and import results straight into the project, without leaving the editor.
- Ships two vetted sources: **Poly Haven** (models and PBR textures, entire catalogue CC0) and **Khronos glTF Sample Assets** (reference models, per-model licenses).
- Downloaded glTF bundles are re-linked and re-exported as a single canonical GLB; FBX sources go through the existing FBX → GLB Importer plugin, preserving the source; texture sets import their Diffuse, Normal, Roughness, AO, Metal and Displacement maps.
- Every result card shows its license, author(s), triangle count, real-world dimensions and maximum texture resolution, plus a link to the original page. A card with a broken preview keeps its identity and specs instead of disappearing.
- Assets whose license is not public domain, or cannot be resolved, require an explicit confirmation that states the terms; they are never imported silently. Assets carrying several licenses report the strictest one.
- Sources are a plain registry: each is one self-contained object, and deleting it removes the source completely. The inclusion rules (CORS, license clarity, no API key) are documented in code and in [Asset Scout](docs/ASSET_SCOUT.md).
- ambientCG and HDRIs are deliberately excluded, with the reasons documented: the former sends no CORS header, the latter has no import path in the engine.

### First Person Pawn and FPS Shooter Test level

- Added a complete **first-person rig** for generic Character Pawns: eye camera, mouse and stick look with pitch clamping, aim down sights, view bob, recoil and a configurable hitscan weapon with magazine, reload, reserve ammo and multi-pellet support.
- Added the `Template - Player Character (First Person)` and `Template - Shooting Target` Logic Element templates, plus a **First Person** node pack (view, weapon, damageable targets) and five weapon events on the shared Pawn event channel.
- Added an optional first-person HUD: crosshair whose gap tracks live spread, hit and kill markers, and an ammo readout.
- Added the **FPS Shooter Test** level template, *Blackpine Urban Training Facility*: a dressed, fully editable environment of roughly 940 objects in twelve outliner zones — a covered staging bay with weapon lockers, prep benches, ammo crates and a lit briefing board; a sandbag firing line built bag by bag; a CQB village with ribbed shipping containers, a walk-in two-room block house with window openings, a doorway and a breach hole with spilled rubble, a wrecked car, tyre stacks and oil drums; a long range with earth berms, wooden target frames and a roofed watchtower; and a fenced perimeter with floodlight masts and signage.
- Twelve damageable targets from 10 m to 65 m, with health, respawn delay and points scaling by distance, including two elevated targets best engaged from the watchtower.
- Kept the level within the project's rendering budget: only five point lights are real, while every other fixture is an unlit emissive lens. The limit is enforced by a test.

### First person: input and camera fixes found in play

- Fixed the first-person camera pointing 180° away from the direction of travel. A Three.js camera looks down its own `-Z` while an engine heading of `yaw` faces `+Z`; the missing half-turn made every control — forward, strafe and turn — read as inverted at once. Mouse and stick look now also follow the engine sign conventions: right turns right, and pushing down looks down.
- Fixed weapon recoil pushing the view down instead of up, a consequence of the same pitch-sign error.
- Fixed the weapon never firing, aiming or reloading. Character templates drive their Pawn from their own graph every frame, and the movement node has no pins for combat, so each frame replaced the whole input command with a movement-only one and zeroed the trigger. `setMoveInput` now passes through the device channels a graph did not author — look, fire, aim and reload — while an explicitly authored value still wins, so a graph can still hold fire on purpose. This affected any Pawn driven from a graph, not just the first-person one.
- Added `fire`, `aim` and `reload` input actions to the Character input context, rebindable like any other. Mouse buttons travel through the keyboard scheme as synthetic `Mouse0`/`Mouse1`/`Mouse2` codes, so they share the existing rebinding interface. They stay unbound in the Vehicle context.

### First person: the visible weapon

- Added a **visible weapon**: `js/runtime/fps-view-model.js` builds a first-person weapon procedurally from primitives, shaped by the active preset, with aim-down-sights, sway, walking bob, recoil kick and a muzzle flash. Previously the weapon existed only as numbers.
- Fixed that weapon never appearing in play. The gameplay render pass hides every object flagged as an editor helper, and the view model carried that flag, so it was culled from exactly the frames it exists for. Objects that live only while the game runs are now marked `runtimeVisual` and survive the pass, which the pass had no way to express before.

### Verticality, and the level fixes it needed

- Made the character controller **height aware**, which is what verticality needed. A box collider is now ignored when the character passes fully under it, the ground solver returns the highest collidable surface at the character's position, and any rise within `stepHeight` is climbed instead of blocked. Stairs, kerbs, decks and container roofs work with no dedicated logic; `characterGroundHeight` had been called but implemented nowhere. Three new movement options — `height`, `stepHeight` and `walkOnColliders` — control it, and switching `walkOnColliders` off restores the previous flat/slope-only behaviour.
- Restored a real climbable staircase to the watchtower, replacing the fixed ladder that stood in for traversal the engine could not do.
- Fixed the player spawning inside the perimeter wall. The collider only ignored a box when the character was above it, so the covered bay roof acted as a full-height wall and displaced the spawn 8 m. Height awareness removed the cause, and the level-side workaround that stripped collision from overhead geometry went with it.
- Fixed the watchtower stair railings sloping against the flight instead of along it, leaving the handrail buried in the upper steps and floating over the lower ones. The rails and their posts are now derived from the stair geometry rather than placed by hand.
- Fixed seven of the twelve targets being impossible to hit: earth berms had been placed across the range in front of the very targets they were meant to back, and the containers and block house stood in the firing lanes. Berms now run along the flanks, containers sit outside the outermost lane and the block house closes the north end.
- Fixed collidable geometry that relied on a rotation the collider does not read. The container builder now takes an axis instead of a rotation, and the wrecked car keeps its angle on non-colliding visuals with a hidden axis-aligned collision proxy.
- Fixed all five point lights in the FPS level rendering black: their colour was written as a CSS string, and the store applies light colour with `Color.setHex`, which floors its argument to `NaN`. Light colours are numeric, and a test now enforces it.

### Character Sound Sets and the Character Sound Designer

- Added **Character Sound Sets**: on-foot audio — footsteps, weapons and body foley — as a saved, reusable set, the on-foot counterpart of the vehicle Engine Sound Sets.
- Everything is **procedural by default**, not as a fallback. The shipped set references no media files and is already a complete soundscape; any slot accepts a sample, and clearing the field returns that slot to its recipe. A wrong path is never silence.
- **Footsteps follow the surface underfoot**: concrete, marble/tile, wood, metal, gravel, dirt, grass, sand, snow, water and carpet, each a distinct recipe rather than one sound retuned — metal rings, gravel is several grains, water has a pitched splash. A test asserts the timbres are actually distinct.
- The material comes from the collider the character stands on, tagged with `surface` on the collider or its scene object and round-tripped through the scene format; untagged geometry uses the set's default. Names are free-form, so a project can invent its own materials and add matching slots.
- Steps are spaced by **distance walked** with a separate walk and run stride, so cadence follows speed at every gait with no case per animation state.
- **Weapons have a profile per class** — rifle, marksman, shotgun, pistol, SMG — each with shot, tail, action, casing, dry fire and both halves of a reload. The class comes from the weapon preset, or from behaviour for a fully custom loadout, never from its display name. It rides the weapon events already on the Pawn event channel, so it needs no wiring per project.
- Added **body foley**: jump, landing scaled by impact speed, and sprint breathing.
- Added the **Character Sound Designer** (Tools menu), loaded on demand: set library, per-slot volume/pitch/randomisation, sample loading and a preview button on every row that plays through the real runtime path, so the editor never grows a second copy of the synthesis.
- Attached to the Character Pawn rather than to a camera mode, so first and third person are audible through the same path. The FPS Shooter Test level tags its walkable geometry, so the watchtower deck, its stairs and the container roofs already sound like metal.
- Generalised the sound-set library in the store into one factory serving both families instead of a second copy of the same CRUD.
- Known limits: only a possessed Pawn is audible — remote and AI characters need spatialisation, which the set does not model yet.

### FPS mode: traversal, items, interaction and vitals

The first-person addition grew from "a camera and a hitscan weapon" into a complete on-foot
gameplay layer. Each piece is a separate opt-in module composed onto the Character Pawn, so a Pawn
that does not declare one keeps the untouched path.

- Added **traversal abilities** (`js/runtime/character-abilities.js`), one state machine shared by
  first and third person: crouch with a headroom check, slow walk, slide, roll, vault, mantle,
  ladder and wall climbing, and an Assassin's-Creed-style **ledge hang** with lateral shuffle and
  pull-up. Obstacle classification reads the same arcade box colliders the movement controller
  resolves against, so an obstacle is vaultable exactly when it is solid — there is no second,
  divergent collision world.
- Slide and roll are **one gesture**: double-tap the Dodge key, and the speed the character is
  already moving at picks the move. Above `minSpeed` it slides, below it rolls. The roll's tumble
  is applied to the body, never to the view.
- Added **character vitals** (`js/runtime/character-vitals.js`): health, armour, stamina, delayed
  regeneration, death and respawn. Vitals mirror their health onto `userData.damageable`, the same
  contract the hitscan resolver writes for a target board, so **the player is damaged by exactly
  the code that damages everything else** and armour rules live in one place.
- Added **world items** (`js/runtime/item-system.js`). An object in the scene *is* an item when it
  carries `userData.item = {kind, ...}` — `weapon`, `health`, `armor`, `ammo` or `custom`. The
  visual is whatever the object already is, so any primitive, GLB or FBX becomes a pickup with no
  second code path.
- Added **world interactions** (`js/runtime/interaction-system.js`), driven by one key. An object
  becomes interactive by carrying `userData.interact = {type, ...}`: `door` (swinging or sliding,
  moving its collider with it), `ladder`, `carry`, `dropZone`, `button` or `climb`. Focus
  resolution — a look ray first, proximity as the fallback — is **shared by the HUD prompt and by
  the verb**, so the prompt and the key can never disagree.
- **Use also climbs.** With nothing to use in front, the same key vaults, mantles, grabs a
  climbable face or catches a ledge, so a player never has to know in advance which of the two a
  given wall is.

### FPS mode: the inventory is a project setting

- The **shape** of the inventory is authored rather than hard-coded, so one runtime serves three
  kinds of game: `none` (one weapon, a pickup replaces what is in hand, consumables used where they
  lie — arena shooter), `slots` (N weapon slots cycled with the swap key — military shooter) and
  `backpack` (slots plus a pack that stores consumables for later, spent with the Use Item key —
  survival and RPG shaped).
- Each slot parks its own magazine and reserve while another weapon is equipped, so a half-empty
  magazine survives a swap. A full loadout **swaps rather than refusing** a pickup, and the weapon
  that leaves is spawned back into the level instead of being destroyed. `allowDrop:false` makes
  the loadout fixed.
- Two verbs, two meanings: **Pick Up takes** an item — a weapon into the inventory, a medkit into
  health or into the pack — while **Use lifts** it and carries it somewhere else, exactly like a
  level crate, because every item quietly gains the same `carry` contract a prop has.
  `carryable:false` makes an item pick-up-only. **Drop** places the weapon in hand at your feet on
  a tap and throws it when held.
- Weapons are therefore droppable, swappable objects rather than a fixed field of the rig, which is
  what makes the same runtime serve a looter and a fixed-loadout shooter.

### FPS mode: third person is not a fallback

- The rig now owns **both** cameras. Third person is not the generic follow camera with the rig
  switched off: it is the rig's own over-the-shoulder camera with the same look angles, the same
  recoil, the same crosshair, the same weapon and the same hitscan down the centre of the screen.
  The only thing that changes is the transform the rig hands to `lot-king.js`. `C` switches; both
  views are equally playable, and `firstPerson.view = 'third'` ships a third-person shooter from
  the same template without touching a node.
- Aiming pulls the camera in over the shoulder instead of only narrowing the field of view, and a
  short wall check pulls it in rather than letting it clip through geometry.
- Body visibility has three modes: hidden (cheapest), `showLegs` — head and shoulders culled so
  looking down shows a real body — and fully visible.
- The weapon is drawn twice, never at once: the procedural view model in front of the camera in
  first person, and an ordinary-depth copy carried by the character in third person. That copy
  takes its **position** from the right hand and its **orientation** from the view angles.
  Parenting it outright to a hand bone is a trap: the grip axis of a hand bone is whatever the rig
  author decided, so a fixed local rotation points the barrel backwards for every rig but one, and
  the bone's scale is inherited. The socket (`firstPerson.weaponSocket`) is authorable — bone name,
  offset, rotation, scale — with a three-axis helper to place it by eye.
- Carrying a weapon is an **upper-body pose laid over whatever the legs are doing**, not a separate
  animation state, so a character running with a rifle still swings its arms. Firing and reloading
  are dispatched as ordinary actions, exactly like the Character and Soccer packs, so binding a
  clip to the `fire` or `reload` animation slot replaces the procedural pose with no code change.

### FPS mode: arms, sights and the full HUD

- Added **arms** to the first-person weapon: hands, forearms and sleeves welded to the grip and the
  foregrip, so every sway, kick and aim blend moves hands and weapon together. The support hand
  leaves the foregrip for the magazine well during a reload.
- Added **telescopic sights** as a property of the *weapon*, not of the HUD: the rig owns the
  magnification and the field of view it implies, and the overlay only draws what the rig reports —
  a HUD that applied its own zoom would be lying about where the bullet goes. The marksman preset
  ships a 4x/8x/12x scope cycled on the mouse wheel; look sensitivity divides by the magnification
  so the same mouse movement sweeps the same angle of the world at every setting. The sight picture
  is four stacked CSS layers and costs no render target.
- Rebuilt the HUD into a full shooter readout: crosshair and hit markers, weapon name, ammo,
  reserve, the carried loadout and the backpack, health, armour and stamina bars, a collider-driven
  radar, the Use and Pick Up prompts, pickup toasts and a damage vignette.
- The HUD now mounts **inside `#hud`**, which is already positioned onto the rectangle the camera
  actually renders into. "Centre of the screen" and "centre of the view" are therefore the same
  point by construction — a fixed, window-centred overlay drifts off the crosshair the moment the
  render rect is not the whole window, which is always true inside the editor. It also renders only
  during a running session or Play Preview, never in edit mode.

- Added `pistol` and `smg` weapon presets with matching view-model profiles and audio classes.

### FPS mode: fixes found in play

- Fixed **crouch on `Ctrl` closing the browser tab**. The game runs in a browser: `Ctrl+W` closes
  the tab, `Ctrl+T` opens one and `Alt` focuses the menu bar, and none of them can be cancelled
  from script because the browser handles them above the page — so "crouch and walk forward" ended
  the game. The traversal keys are now modifier-free, and the keyboard source refuses to latch a
  key that arrived as part of a modifier combo, which used to leave an action stuck on after the
  browser swallowed the keyup.
- Fixed **the view snapping round at random**. Pointer lock hands over a fresh coordinate space and
  browsers routinely report one enormous `movementX`/`Y` on the first event after the transition.
  Two frames of warm-up plus a per-event sanity limit remove it with no smoothing lag.
- Fixed **running up stairs forcing a permanent crouch, and launching a slide on every step**. The
  headroom test matched any box between the feet and the head, which is also the next tread of a
  staircase. A ceiling is now a box whose *underside* is above waist height and below standing
  height, so a tread is excluded by construction.
- Fixed **objects rising by half their own height every time a level was saved and reloaded**.
  Material properties travel in two shapes — flat from a template, nested under `global` from a
  saved project — and `createPrimitive` reads geometry keys such as `centered` out of the same bag,
  so the nested shape silently lost them on the round trip. Reading now accepts both shapes, and
  projects already saved with the nested one repair themselves: the stored transform was never
  corrupted, only the mesh offset inside the group.
- Fixed **the muzzle flash leaving the barrel sideways**. The flare cone is modelled along `+Y` and
  turned onto the barrel by a fixed `-90°` about X; the random spin was written to `rotation.z`,
  and Euler `XYZ` applies Z innermost, so it tipped the flame out of the muzzle instead of rotating
  it around its own axis.
- Fixed **the third-person weapon hanging off the hip and ignoring the arm swing** on every
  character without an imported rig. The hand lookup required `isBone`, and the procedural
  placeholder body has no skeleton at all — only named joints.
- Fixed **collision gaps**: tyre stacks had no collider at all (their tyres are decorative tori,
  which never collide), and the watchtower posts, the overwatch railings, the berm crests, the
  container roof rails, the block house roof lip and the wrecked car's cabin were all walk-through.
  The level's collision budget is still well inside the limit its test enforces.
- Fixed **footsteps sounding like struck metal and being far too loud**. The noise bands sat high,
  the material resonances were long and loud, and every voice opened on a fixed 4 ms ramp, which is
  heard as a click. Attack now scales with decay, the recipes are duller and quieter, and crouching
  and slow walking are quieter still.

### Character Sound Sets: assigned per Pawn, tuned by ear

- A Character Sound Set is now assigned **per Pawn** (`characterPawn.soundSet`), the way an engine
  sound set is assigned per vehicle. A guard, a civilian and the player can walk on the same floor
  and sound like three different people. Empty falls back to the level's set, so a project that only
  wants one never has to think about it.
- Fixed the level's set **never being reloaded**. The designer only ever changed the live set, so
  every edit was silently lost on the next session — which is indistinguishable from the editor not
  working. The level now records its set and the runtime reconciles it from the frame loop, which
  also catches the editor's live save, duplicate and switch paths that a scene-apply hook would
  miss.
- Added a **loop button** to every slot row, so a sound is tuned by ear while the sliders move
  instead of by guessing and re-clicking. Exactly one loop runs at a time.
- Moved the designer onto a **toolbar button** next to Add. It was reachable only from `Tools`,
  three levels down a menu, which is the same as not existing. The Weapons tab now opens on the
  class actually in hand.

### Pre-benchmark: spawnable objects are warmed up too

- Weapon models and pickup visuals are built the first time they are needed, which is mid-play — on
  a pickup, a drop or a weapon swap — and the benchmark's scene snapshot never sees them because
  they do not exist yet. A warm-up hook now builds one of every weapon profile in both variants, one
  visual of every pickup kind, and the HUD's DOM and radar canvas, lets the benchmark render and
  compile them, then throws them away before the frame sample. `frustumCulled = false` is what makes
  it work without any camera maths: the point is to compile the programs and upload the buffers, not
  to draw pixels.

### FPS mode: visible rounds, falling objects and the shoulder swap

- Added **visible rounds** (`js/runtime/weapon-tracers.js`): a tracer streak flies from the muzzle
  to the point the shot actually reached, and a hit leaves an impact flash. The module is
  deliberately cosmetic — the hit was resolved before the event it listens to was emitted, so it
  cannot change where a bullet lands. A missed shot still draws, because the trace now reports the
  point the round *would* have reached.
- **What a tracer looks like is the calibre**, so it is weapon data (`weapon.tracer`): speed,
  length, width, colour, how many rounds in N leave a streak, and the impact fade. Defaults are
  derived from damage, so a custom weapon looks right with nothing authored.
- The cost is **fixed, not growing**: the pool is allocated once and the oldest streak is recycled
  when it is full, so a minute of sustained automatic fire costs exactly what the first shot did.
  Geometry is shared and materials are cached per colour.
- Dropped, thrown and shot items now **fall properly**: a small ballistic body with real contacts
  against the arcade box colliders, resolved on the axis of least penetration and put to sleep once
  it is too slow to bounce or slide. **Mass is the dial** — restitution is derived from it, so light
  things bounce and skitter while heavy things land and stay. Shooting an item pushes it with the
  impulse divided by mass, so the round that sends a can flying barely moves a crate.
- Added the **shoulder swap** on `V`. The weapon, the arms and the third-person camera offset all
  mirror; the crosshair never moves, which is the whole point — you change which side of cover you
  can shoot from without changing your aim.
- **Shooting targets are solid, and they no longer vanish.** They were hidden outright on death,
  which is wrong for something that is going to become a physics object; they now fold flat and are
  stood back up on respawn, and Logic Elements can opt into collision per placement.

### FPS mode: aiming fixes found in play

- Fixed **the weapon sitting pointed at the sky after every burst and all through a reload**. The
  model inherits the camera quaternion, where a positive `rotateX` lifts the muzzle — and reload and
  the sprint carry were both written positive, so the two poses that should dip the weapon raised it
  instead. The recoil term was also a second full kick on top of the one already folded into the
  view angles; it is now a small extra lift plus a backward punch toward the shoulder, which is what
  a recoil actually does.
- Fixed **the third-person weapon appearing on the wrong side**. Picking the hand by name is wrong,
  and the procedural body proves it: its `hand_skin_right` joint sits at local `+X`, which under this
  engine's convention (`+Z` forward, so right is `-X`) is the character's *left*. The name describes
  the model author's idea of right, not the runtime's. The side is now decided geometrically, which
  is correct for any rig — and is also what makes the shoulder swap a one-line change instead of a
  second lookup table.
- Fixed **the carry pose reading as a T-pose**. The first version cut the arm splay to bring the
  elbows in, which actually lifted the arms halfway back toward the rest pose. The arms now stay down
  and swing forward, aiming locks the pose while hip carry keeps most of the run swing alive, firing
  punches both arms back and reloading drops the support hand — so running, aiming, firing and
  reloading are each visibly different before a single real clip is bound.
- Fixed **the third-person weapon not kicking with the shot**: it read the view angles, which are the
  player's intent, rather than the aim angles, which carry the live recoil. A new `rig.aimAngles()`
  is the one place that distinction lives.

### FPS mode: the body bends, and things actually fall

- Gave the procedural body **elbows**: each arm is now shoulder → upper arm → elbow → forearm →
  hand. Without them an arm is one rigid bar, and no amount of shoulder posing stops a character
  holding a rifle from reading as a T-pose. The trigger arm folds, the support arm folds further to
  reach the foregrip, and a reload straightens it as it drops away. A project saved before elbows
  existed has no node with those ids, and an absent part is skipped, so nothing breaks.
- Fixed **sprinting keeping the weapon at the hip while it fired**. Running and shooting is a real
  thing to want to do; the carry now yields to the trigger for a window that outlives the cadence,
  so an automatic burst does not strobe the pose. The third-person body treats firing as aiming for
  the same reason.
- Made the **weapon socket authorable one component at a time**
  (`firstPerson.weaponSocket.offsetY`, `.rotationX`, `.bone`, `.scale`, `.showHelper`), so a slider
  or a graph can place the weapon on an imported character without rebuilding the block.
- Fixed **two weapons appearing in hand** after a pickup. A full loadout drops the outgoing weapon,
  and it was spawned on the player's own feet — inside the pickup radius, so it was immediately
  taken back and the two traded places forever. It is now tossed clear, and every fresh drop is
  deaf to Pick Up for a moment.
- Generalised the ballistic body to **anything with mass**, not only pickups. A level crate carries
  an `interact` contract and no `item` one, and a shot target carries neither; physical properties
  are now resolved from whichever contract an object has, so shooting a crate moves it and a killing
  shot knocks its target over. An object with none of them is static level geometry and never moves.
- Releasing a carried object now **lets go** instead of placing it: it falls, bounces and settles
  through the same body. Teleporting it onto the floor was the giveaway that carrying and dropping
  were two unrelated systems. A body that owns a collider now takes it along.
- Added **bullet holes**, capped by their own fixed pool that recycles oldest-first. They need the
  surface normal to lie flat, so the hitscan now reports one; a hit on something with a health pool
  leaves no hole, because a body is not masonry.
- World items, interactions and tracers now run on the game's **time scale**. A bullet at full speed
  through the one moment you slowed down to look at it is the opposite of the point.

### FPS mode: impact, limbs and the flash

- Fixed **shot targets twitching on the spot**. A body was colliding with its OWN collider: every
  frame it pushed itself back out of the box it stands in, and the fix-up alternated axes. A body
  now ignores the colliders it owns.
- Fixed **standing targets being shoved around the range on every hit**. Something with a health
  pool now *absorbs* the shot while it stands and only becomes a falling body once it is down; the
  graph tips it past its balance point and the impulse does the rest.
- **Ctrl can never be bound**, in any context. It is stripped from every scheme including one
  restored from an older project, and rebinding cannot put it back — the page never sees `Ctrl+W`,
  so a binding there is not a control, it is a way to lose the session mid-fight. Alt stays, because
  it *can* be cancelled while the game holds pointer lock.
- Fixed **the arms being posed on the wrong side of the body**. This rig is authored with
  `arm_skin_right` at local `+X`, and the engine's convention is `+Z` forward — so the character's
  right is local `-X` and the node named "right" is on its left. The weapon had already been fixed
  to pick its hand geometrically; the pose had not, so one arm held the weapon while the other was
  posed for it. Both now resolve the side the same way.
- Rebuilt the carry pose around that: the trigger arm hangs near vertical and swings forward onto
  the grip, the support arm swings *past* vertical so it crosses the chest and reaches the foregrip,
  and the elbows fold to match. A sidearm leaves the support arm by the body; a reload straightens
  it as it drops away; firing punches both arms back.
- Fixed **the muzzle flash drawing on top of the weapon it comes out of**. The flash skipped the
  depth test entirely; it now tests depth while the weapon keeps writing it, so the flare is hidden
  by the barrel and the hands in front of it and still passes over the world beyond the muzzle.

### FPS mode: the on-foot control layout settles

- **Use and Pick Up are one key.** `F` tapped uses whatever is in front — a door, a ladder, a crate
  to carry, a ledge to climb — and `F` **held** fills a ring around the key and takes the item. The
  ring is driven by the Pawn's own hold timer, so what fills on screen and what fires the pickup are
  one number rather than two that drift apart. With nothing takeable in front the key acts on press,
  so a door never waits for a timer it does not need.
- Added **leaning** on `Q` and `E`. It is not a tilt effect: the eye actually slides sideways, which
  is the part that lets you see past a corner, and the view rolls with it. The chest, head and
  shoulders follow in third person, so the character peeks rather than sidesteps. The lean is
  stopped by whatever it runs into — sliding the eye through a wall would let a player see into
  rooms they are not standing in. Both keys at once is upright.
- **Crouch is a toggle on `C`**: one press goes down and stays down while you walk and shoot, a
  second press stands up, and **sprinting stands the character up** instead of refusing to run —
  asking for Crouch again before you can sprint is a step that only ever has one answer. A crouch
  forced by a low ceiling still wins, because it has to.
- The rest of the layout moved to make room: the first/third person toggle to `B`, the weapon swap
  to `Z` and the mouse wheel, and the dedicated pickup key is unbound by default (the action still
  exists for a project that wants one). A binding still sitting on an old default is migrated; a
  key the player chose themselves is left alone.

### FPS mode: seven roles, fists, blades and grenades

- Added **weapon kinds**. `kind` is the one field that changes what pulling the trigger means:
  `firearm` hitscans at range and costs a round, `melee` hitscans at arm's length and costs nothing,
  `thrown` leaves the hand as a real object, and `unarmed` is melee with no model in hand. Added
  `fists`, `knife`, `bat`, `flashbang` and `grenade` presets.
- Replaced the flat weapon list with **seven roles on the number row** — fists, sidearm, primary,
  melee, bonus, flashbang, grenade. A slot is a role, not a position, so `3` is always the primary
  weapon whatever order things were picked up in. A pickup takes the first EMPTY role it fits, so a
  second rifle lands in the bonus slot instead of throwing away the one already carried.
- A **thrown weapon is handed to the item system** as an ordinary body with a fuse: it flies,
  bounces and settles like everything else, and on detonation damages everything inside its radius
  with distance falloff and throws whatever has a body outward. One function, so a grenade and a
  scripted explosion cannot disagree about what an explosion does.
- Fixed **the elbows bending on the wrong axis**. The forearm runs along the arm's local X, and
  rotating that about Z swings it sideways — which is why the hands reached out to the left and the
  right instead of forward onto the weapon. It bends on Y now, which the shoulder has already turned
  to face forward, and the elbow flares slightly off the ribs so the forearm has somewhere to go.
- A **one-handed weapon leaves the off arm alone** entirely: a pistol is held in one hand and the
  other arm keeps swinging with the run, which is what it does in life.
- **Dropped items look like what they are.** Weapons use the real weapon geometry, ammo is an olive
  crate, medkits and armour are cased with a coloured face, and a thrown object is a dark body with
  a lit band. White boxes told the player nothing.

### FPS mode: the weapon wheel and the backpack

- Added `js/runtime/inventory-ui.js`: `I` tapped opens a **weapon wheel** laid out as the seven
  roles in a circle, `I` held opens the **backpack**. Same tap-versus-hold rule as the Use key, so
  it is learned once rather than twice.
- The wheel is **steered with the mouse without releasing pointer lock** — flicking toward a weapon
  must not also turn the character, and showing a cursor would stop the game being a game. The
  look path hands the delta to the wheel while it is open and to the view otherwise.
- Highlighting **equips live** rather than on a confirm press: a wheel that needs a second press is
  slower than the number key it replaces. An empty role still shows, so the layout never reflows
  under the pointer, and the number keys keep working throughout.
- Mounted inside `#hud` like the rest of the shooter overlay, so the wheel is centred on the
  rendered view rather than on the window — the only thing that puts it on the crosshair inside the
  editor viewport.

### FPS mode: the support hand reaches the weapon

- The support arm is now **solved onto the foregrip**, not posed toward it. The view model reports
  where the grip actually is on the weapon it is drawing, and a two-bone IK pass aims the shoulder
  and folds the elbow so the hand lands there — the same point a rigged GLB will attach its hand to.
  The two mirrored elbow solutions are both evaluated and the one that reaches is kept, which is far
  more robust than deriving the sign through three coordinate conventions.
- Aiming now **extends the arm** instead of swinging a folded one forward: the elbow opens as the
  weapon goes out to arm's length.
- Fixed **the body leaning the opposite way from the camera** in third person. The character faces
  `+Z`, so its right is `-X`, and rotating the torso's up vector about `+Z` sends it toward `-X` —
  leaning right is a positive rotation. The first version negated it.
- Fixed **`C` still popping the view-mode message** while on foot. A raw `keydown` handler in
  `lot-king.js` read the letter directly and bypassed the binding entirely, so it kept firing on the
  vehicle's key after the on-foot one moved to `B`. The same handler was also flashing a vehicle's
  headlights on `F`, which is the Use key on foot.

### Procedural surfaces, and the FPS level dressed with them

- Added **`js/engine/procedural-surfaces.js`**, an engine service that turns an editor primitive's
  flat colour into a real material. Eighteen surface kinds — concrete, plaster, asphalt, dirt,
  gravel, sand, brick, cinderblock, wood, plywood, sandbag, tarp, rubber, painted / corrugated /
  rusted / tread metal — are generated at runtime from canvas as an albedo, a Sobel-derived normal
  map and, where it earns its memory, a roughness map. No binary assets, nothing fetched.
- A primitive opts in with one prop: `props.surfaceTexture = {kind, tile, seed, rotate, strength}`.
  `tile` is **metres of world per repeat**, so a 52 m wall and a 0.6 m crate get the same texel
  density, and the repeat is recomputed from the live scale rather than baked at creation.
- The generated albedo is neutral grain that **multiplies** the authored colour, so an object's
  colour still drives its look and only the grain comes from the surface. Texture sets are cached
  and shared, per-object tiling uses texture clones over one shared GPU upload, and the disposal
  paths know not to free a shared set when one object using it is deleted.
- Fixed dressed materials failing to draw at all: a material that gains its maps inside
  `createPrimitive` ends up with a program that does not match the samplers it binds, and WebGL
  reports `glDrawElements: Mismatch between texture format and sampler type`. In the FPS level that
  silently removed the ground, the boundary walls and the bay roof from the image. Dressed
  materials are now recompiled once, a couple of frames after the scene is live.
- **The FPS Shooter Test level is re-dressed on top of it.** Every piece of geometry names a
  material class — colour, roughness, metalness, procedural surface, world tile size and the
  footstep material the Character Sound Set plays — instead of carrying a loose hex, and the
  footstep surface is derived from the class rather than typed per object.
- Corrugation now lives in the surface rather than in geometry: the containers lost 22 rib boxes
  each and the boundary wall 48, and the objects that freed paid for corner castings, door
  hardware, razor wire, a loadout table, cable spools, steel gongs, ground decals and a new
  `12 Outside the Wire` silhouette layer of pine treelines, a water tower and a radio mast.
- Zones renumbered `01`–`12` in walking order. There used to be two `08`s, which left the Practice
  Yard and the Characters folder in arbitrary outliner order; a test now enforces unique, sorted
  numbering.
- The level authors its own light: a 24° raking sun, a hemisphere fill high enough that the shaded
  side of a container stays readable, restrained sun bloom and lens flare, fog density and a colour
  grade. Fog and grade are **camera** values — `sky.js` rewrites `scene.fog` every frame — so the
  `env.fog` block this template used to carry was never read by anything and is gone.
- Fixed three faults the flat-shaded version hid: a container's "roof rail" was built a full
  container tall and rendered as a black slab between the stacked pair; every torus prop was
  authored as if the scale were its radius, when a torus primitive's visible outer radius is 1.8×
  its scale, so the tyre stacks were two-metre donuts; and a bay pillar stood dead in the
  crosshair of a player who had not moved yet. Tests cover all three.

### Performance: render resolution, texture budget and shadow cost

Three ceilings the engine never had, all three found by measuring rather than by feel.

- **Render resolution had no limit.** The pixel ratio is the product of four multipliers — device
  DPR, quality preset, supersampling ratio and session render scale — and nothing clamped the
  result: `High` + SSAA 2× on a 2× DPR panel asked for 2.83× per axis (eight times the pixels of the
  window) and `Extreme` + SSAA 4× was allowed up to 4× per axis, or sixteen times. A 2560 px
  viewport was rendering past 8000 px wide and then being downscaled onto the panel — all of the
  fill rate, none of the resolution. The policy is now one pure, exported function with three
  ceilings: an effective ratio of 2, an absolute 4K buffer budget on the long axis and in total, and
  a floor of 0.5 so a downgrade stays readable. Supersampling still helps where it helps — a low-DPR
  display — and stops buying detail a high-DPR panel already has.
- **Added a manual Render resolution control** (50%–200% of the window), independent of the quality
  preset. There is one renderer behind the editor viewport, the editor Play preview and the
  standalone game, so the setting governs all three. The row reports the buffer the renderer
  *actually* got, in pixels and megapixels, including when the budget clamps the request — a
  resolution control that hides its own result is how the 8000 px viewport went unnoticed.
- **Added a Texture budget**, `js/engine/texture-budget.js` plus a Video setting: the largest texture
  the engine uploads, 256 px to 4096 px, defaulting to a deliberate 1024 px. Imported images, decals
  and the maps embedded in a GLB are downscaled as they load; the source files on disk and in the
  project are never touched, so raising the cap restores full detail on the next load. Data,
  compressed and video textures are skipped rather than mangled. The adaptive-low fallback pulls the
  cap down to 512 px. For scale: a 4K map is 67 MB before mipmaps, five maps to a full PBR material.
- **Shadow casting is now opt-out per object.** Every primitive cast a shadow, including ground
  decals lying flat on the surface they stain and silhouettes 100 m outside the play area — each one
  redrawn into the shadow map every frame. `props.castShadow:false` turns it off for geometry that
  cannot contribute a visible shadow, and the FPS level uses it for its decals, its floors and the
  whole `12 Outside the Wire` layer: **914 casters down to 753**, with no visible difference.

Measured cost of the FPS Shooter Test level as it ships, all GPU-independent: 950 objects, 1039
meshes, ~810 inside the frustum at the spawn, 753 shadow casters, 119 textures, 65 shader programs
and 32.7 MB of procedural texture memory across 41 generated surface sets.

### Items look like what they are on the ground

- A pickup authored in a level can now ask for the same model the item system gives a **dropped**
  item, with `item.visual:'auto'`. The authored object stays the editable one — transform, contract
  and save all live on it — and simply stops drawing its placeholder.
- The FPS Shooter Test level uses it for every pickup, so the weapons on the loadout table are
  weapons instead of white boxes, and match exactly what you see if you throw one on the floor.

### FPS mode: what is still open

Documented honestly rather than deferred silently:

- **No impact decals.** A hit leaves a brief flash, not a mark that stays on the surface.
- **A knocked-down target is a rigid body, not a ragdoll.** It tips and falls as one piece; there
  are no joints to break.
- **Item contacts are spheres against boxes**, not a rigid-body solver. Enough for dropping,
  throwing and shooting loose objects; not a stack of crates that settles.
- **Carried objects pass through geometry** — their collider is disabled while held.
- **Traversal is a position tween**, not root motion. It reads correctly and never fights the
  collision solver, but the character does not physically reach for the ledge unless a `vault`,
  `mantle` or `climb` clip is bound.
- **The third-person weapon does not aim with the arms.** It follows the hand and points where the
  player looks, but there is no aim-offset pose layer.
- **The scope has no real barrel distortion**: the curvature is a gradient and a chromatic ring, not
  a lens shader.
- **The radar draws colliders, not meshes**, so anything without a collider is invisible on it.
- Only a **possessed Pawn is audible**; remote and AI characters need spatialisation.
- **The view model is still the roughest thing on screen.** The arms and weapon are built from
  primitives and, now that the level around them is dressed, they are the next single biggest visual
  gap — not the environment.
- **The HUD lists a duplicate entry** for the combat knife and the frag grenade at spawn, floating
  mid-screen.
- **Volumetric clouds are not usable from a level.** Enabling `env.volClouds` composites the cloud
  layer over the whole scene and hides the ground and the walls; the FPS template leaves them off.
- **Dressed materials are recompiled once, two frames after a level loads.** That is a documented
  workaround, not a root cause: attaching maps inside `createPrimitive` leaves the renderer with a
  program that does not match the samplers it binds. Texture format, colour spaces, source sharing,
  the shadow samplers and the pre-benchmark warm-up were each ruled out by isolation; what the first
  compile actually gets wrong is still unknown.
